Catch Shares Versus Race-to-Fish

Same ocean, same fish, same biology. Change only the rule about who may catch the fish, and you get three very different fisheries.

Three rules, one fish stock

This is a simple fishery model. The fish grow by the same rule in every case. The only thing that changes is the management rule:

  • Open access. Anyone can fish, with no limit on the catch.
  • Seasonal quota. The regulator caps the total catch and closes the season when the cap is hit. Nobody owns a share, so every boat races for it. Fishermen call this a derby.
  • Catch shares. The same total cap, but each boat owns a tradable share of it (an individual tradable quota), so there is nothing to race for.

The numbers

Did this happen anywhere real?

Alaska's Pacific halibut fishery ran as a derby until 1995, then switched to individual quotas with the same kind of catch cap.

How this was built

I asked an AI (Claude) to build a standard Gordon-Schaefer fishery model, solve it for the long-run outcome under each rule, and draw the charts. I chose the question, the three rules, and the round-number inputs. The page recomputes everything in your browser, so you can read the code with View Source.

  • Biology (identical in all three): yearly growth = 0.4 × stock × (1 − stock ÷ 100), stock in thousand tons. The stock can replace at most 10 thousand tons a year, when it sits at 50.
  • Catch per boat per day: 0.02 tons for every thousand tons of fish in the water (1 ton a day when the stock is 50).
  • Costs: a running cost per day fished, plus $60,000 a year to own a boat. The fishing window is 200 good-weather days.
  • Open access: boats keep entering until an average boat earns zero profit.
  • Seasonal quota: catch capped at 10 thousand tons. Boats keep entering until the race shortens the season enough that an average boat earns zero profit.
  • Catch shares: the same cap, caught by the smallest fleet that can do it in the full window.
  • Safety is an assumption, not a result: I assume 1 day in 5 has bad weather, and that only derby boats go out in it, because a day in port is fish lost to a rival. The halibut rescue numbers above are the real-world check on that assumption.

What the model leaves out: prices that rise when fish is sold fresh all year, who gets the shares for free, crew jobs lost when the fleet shrinks, and cheating. Good next questions: who captured the rent in Alaska, and what happened to the towns that lost boats?
