Plastic Bag Bans, Before and After

Bag bans and bag fees do what they say: the thin plastic bag mostly disappears. The catch is what shoppers pick up instead.

2. But shoppers replace it with something

When California cities banned thin plastic carryout bags, an economist tracked what stores sold next. People had been reusing the free bags as bin liners, so they bought trash bags. Stores handed out paper.

3. And a loophole can undo the whole thing

Total tonnage rose 47%, from 157,385 to 231,072 tons.

Why it went up

The 2014 law banned thin bags but let stores sell thicker plastic bags as "reusable" for 10 cents.

Each thick bag holds several times the plastic of a thin one, and few were reused. California passed a second law in 2024 that bans them too, starting in 2026.

What I take from this

A bag policy is judged on three different things, and the answer changes with each one. On litter, it works. On the number of thin bags, it works dramatically. On total plastic and total material, it depends on what the law lets shoppers switch to. Ireland charged for every plastic bag and left no thick-bag exit. California left one, and the plastic came back heavier.

How this was built

I asked an AI (Claude) to find before-and-after measurements of plastic bag bans and fees, pull the exact figures, and lay them out as charts. Nothing here is a new estimate: every number is copied from the sources below, and the bars are drawn to scale within each chart. The only arithmetic is the 47% (231,072 ÷ 157,385).

Read with care:

  • Pounds of paper and pounds of plastic are not equal harm. Paper is heavier, breaks down, and is recycled more, but takes more energy and water to make. The chart shows weight only.
  • The California tonnage comes from an advocacy group's reading of state waste studies, it covers all plastic grocery and merchandise bags, and the years include the pandemic, when reusable bags were briefly discouraged.
  • The beach study measures the share of bags in litter, not the total amount, and compares places with and without policies.
  • Ireland's figures are national estimates reported by the government.

Good next questions: how many times does a thick bag or a cotton tote have to be reused to beat a thin bag? Does a fee work better than a ban? What happened in New York after its 2020 ban?
