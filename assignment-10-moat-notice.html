The University of Rochester Should Keep Thinking Human

What UR should do: Require handwritten, in-class final exams in every major, plus one live oral defense each year. No laptops, no AI. Call it the Meliora Viva.

What it's fighting: Every college is racing to "integrate AI." The pressure comes from students who want speed, employers who want AI fluency, rankings that reward growth, and peer schools that will call this backward. Our moat is refusing. A UR degree would mean a person did the thinking.

Why it matters: In-person exams are one of the last tools a school has to find out what a student actually learned and can do. That's especially true for theory- and argument-heavy fields like economics and the social sciences, where defending an idea out loud shows understanding a typed essay no longer proves. Students need a way to show their abilities, and in-person testing is one of the only ones left.

The most visible cost: student stress and lost convenience, paid by students. It is not the largest cost. That is faculty time: grading handwriting and running oral exams for every undergrad takes hours away from research and teaching. Faculty pay it first, and tuition pays it eventually.

We expect pushback:

  • It's harder on students with disabilities. Accommodations stay.
  • It may scare off some applicants. Maybe those aren't the students this moat is for.
  • Any school could copy it. But few will pay the cost, and that's what makes it a real signal.

Ask yourself: If any school could copy a tradition but almost none are willing to pay for it, is it still a moat? And would it make Rochester more itself, or just harder to belong to?
